What it does

Light, quick and out of your way.

Tuck lives in your menu bar and opens with one shortcut.

Remembers what you copy

Text, links, images and files. Search as you type and pin the things you need again and again.

Keyboard first

Open with ⇧⌘V, press ↩ to paste, or ⌘1 to ⌘9 to paste the one you want.

Paste queue

Line up several items, then paste them one by one, oldest first, with ⌃⌘V.

Spots secrets

API keys, tokens, private keys and card numbers are hidden and removed after a few minutes, and the clipboard is cleared after you paste one.

Respects password managers

Skips anything marked as concealed or transient, plus any apps you choose to ignore.

Preview and history

Press ⌘Y to see the full text, image or file list, with where it came from and when.

Private by design

Nothing leaves your Mac.

Tuck has no network code and no telemetry. Your clipboard history is stored in an encrypted file on your Mac, with the key in your login Keychain.

  • No network codeTuck never connects to the internet.
  • No telemetryNo analytics, no crash reports, no accounts.
  • Open sourceRead every line on GitHub. MIT licence.

Questions

Good to know.

Is Tuck free?

Yes. Tuck is free and open source under the MIT licence, with no ads and no account.

Why does macOS warn me when I open it?

The preview isn't signed by Apple yet. The download page explains the one-time "Open Anyway" step in System Settings, or you can build it yourself from the source.

Which Macs does it need?

macOS 14 Sonoma or later. Windows preview builds are also attached to the releases page.

Does Tuck send my clipboard anywhere?

No. Tuck has no network code, so your clipboard history can't leave your Mac.

It's a preview. What should I expect?

The core works and has unit tests, but it hasn't had much real-world use yet, so expect rough edges. Please report problems on GitHub, or email arcova.apps@gmail.com.
